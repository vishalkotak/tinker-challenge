# Score every RL recipe against the exact optimum

The strongest project is a **"Lipogram-KL" benchmark**. Define the target π*(y|x) ∝ p_base(y|x)·exp(-λ·e_count(y)/β), then score each RL recipe on Tinker by its exact distance to that target in nats, not by reward. The scan found no paper that does this at real-LLM scale. Distribution matching toward an explicit target stopped at GPT-2 with word-inclusion constraints ([Korbak et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00761)). The only head-to-head KL table covering PPO and DPG ran on 33M to 355M models ([Twisted SMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). Distance to π* has otherwise been measured only in a tabular toy ([AGRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19612v2)). Three of the other candidates are not rivals. They come out of the same runs as chapters. The "shrinkage is optimal" decomposition is a prediction you can register before training, and it explains the shrinking answers Wu could not fix with any reward change ([Wu](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). Masked-teacher on-policy distillation becomes one more entrant, and its myopia bias shows up as a number. Rank 1 vs rank 32 tests "LoRA Without Regret" against ground truth rather than reward ([LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). The two-axis Gao curves are a good second act if budget remains. The planning-horizon study is the riskiest and should be cut first. This suits Schulman in particular. He co-wrote the overoptimization scaling laws ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)), and the lab ships on-policy distillation on Tinker ([On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). The core benchmark needs no judge calls, so it fits $25 better than anything judge-based.

## The lipogram is a rare RL task with a computable optimum

KL-regularized RL toward a reward r has a closed-form optimum, p_base·exp(r/β)/Z. Korbak, Perez and Buckley framed that as variational inference. Their paper was not re-fetched this round ([arXiv 2205.11275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11275)). For most rewards, the framing tells you nothing usable, because r is a learned model and Z cannot be computed. The lipogram is different. Set r = -λ·e_count. Then every term of the objective is exact: e_count is a regex, and Tinker exposes base and policy logprobs through `compute_logprobs` ([On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)).

That gives one fact the whole project rests on. My derivation, not from a paper: **KL(πθ‖π*) = log Z - ELBO**, where ELBO = E_πθ[log p_base + r/β - log πθ] is estimated from the policy's own samples. Log Z depends only on the prompt and β, not on the algorithm. So **ranking algorithms by reverse KL to the optimum needs no estimate of Z at all**. Z matters only for the absolute number and for the forward direction.

For absolute numbers, the target supports bounds in both directions. Importance sampling from the base gives a stochastic lower bound on log Z. For the hard constraint there is a better proposal: mask every e-containing token. The importance weight then equals the product of the allowed probability mass at each step, which gives an unbiased estimator of Z. That is my inference, consistent with the SMC-steering literature ([Loula et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13139)). An upper bound needs exact target samples. Zhao et al. get those by rejection sampling, which works when the likelihood ratio is bounded, as it is for indicator targets with the base model as proposal. The gap between the two bounds then bounds the symmetrized KL ([Twisted SMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)).

The prior work fixes where the gap is. Twisted SMC is the closest analog. On sentiment, its contrastive twists scored 0.55 and 0.47 nats in the two KL directions, while PPO scored 1.04±0.31 and 0.87±0.20. On infilling, DPG won with 13.27 and 4.90 ([Twisted SMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). Those models were TinyStories-33M and GPT2-Medium. AGRO showed that off-policy KL-regularized policy gradient misses π*, but only in a tabular toy. Its Llama 3 8B runs report accuracy alone ([AGRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19612v2)). "A Comedy of Estimators" compared KL estimator choices (k1, k2, k3, placed in the reward or in the loss) on Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B, scoring them by stability and downstream accuracy. The abstract does not report distance to π* ([arXiv 2512.21852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21852)). f-DPG found "no universally optimal" divergence for matching a target ([arXiv 2302.08215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08215)). So the question "which estimator and which loss actually land nearest the optimum they claim to target?" has never had an exact answer at 4B scale. The lipogram can give one.

The lexical angle is also new. GDC-style work used *include word w* constraints. Letter avoidance touches nearly every token. The scan found no paper that uses a lipogram as a posterior or RL target, though that search covered abstracts only ([Twisted SMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546); [Loula et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13139)).

**Rank 1 is the spine because it turns a toy task into a measuring instrument.** Every RL paper reports reward. This one reports how far each recipe lands from the distribution it is supposed to converge to. GRPO's group normalization and per-token k3 KL in the loss do not optimize the sequence-level objective whose optimum is π* (my inference). The drift can be measured as a fixed number of nats per variant. The benchmark can also test a Gao claim, made with Schulman, that the KL penalty "mostly acts like early stopping", which the authors themselves flagged as possibly sensitive to hyperparameters ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)). Vary β at matched KL-from-init and see whether the runs land at the same distance from the optimum.

**Rank 2 is the hook.** Under the hard constraint, the posterior reweights the base length distribution by the chance that a sequence of length L avoids "e". That chance decays roughly geometrically in L, so the exact target prefers short answers. The soft penalty does the same (my derivation). This is computable *before any training*: E_π*[L] = E_base[L·w]/E_base[w], with w = exp(-λ·e_count/β) over base samples. Wu saw answers shrink into half-answers and found that "no reward change fixed" it ([Wu](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). The project can register a predicted length, train, and then show which part of the drop the objective demands and which part is the algorithm's excess. Nobody in the scan has measured the length of π* against a trained policy, and a targeted search found no source for the claim ([DRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04474) derives a Gibbs optimum only for explicit length rewards). The second message is a design lesson: if you want "same answer, no e", shrinkage is a property of the target, not of RL, and you fix it in the target.

**Rank 3 is cheap and on-brand.** Lab work showed on-policy distillation reaching AIME'24 70% in about 150 steps, 9 to 30x cheaper than extrapolated SFT, and it is a one-line change to the Tinker RL loop ([On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). A masked teacher has trivial logprobs: base logprobs renormalized over e-free tokens. But masking is myopic. On Mistral-7B, token-masked lipogram decoding produced misspellings like "investiging" and Cyrillic lookalikes, and human quality fell from 4.68 to 3.56 ([AprAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01103v2)). Loula et al. describe that bias in general ([Loula et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13139)). The prediction is that OPD converges fast and cheaply to the *wrong* distribution, and the benchmark puts a number on how wrong. That number is the value of lookahead, in nats. Self-distilled twisted SMC tried SMC-to-weights amortization only on TinyStories-33M, with no compute comparison ([arXiv 2507.02315](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02315)). One trap: SDFT's repo now says its results used forward KL, not the reverse KL that secondary sources describe ([idanshen/Self-Distillation](https://github.com/idanshen/Self-Distillation)). State which divergence you use.

**Rank 4 is the best fit with Schulman's own work, but the crowd is closer.** Gao's closed forms are R_bon(d) = d(α - β·d) and R_RL(d) = d(α - β·log d), with d = sqrt(KL), using a 6B gold RM. BoN's KL is analytic: log n - (n-1)/n ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)). In 2026, Liu et al. showed policies hacking non-reasoning judges against a gpt-oss-120b gold ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12246)). Wang tied judge-hacking depth to judge AUC (Spearman -0.69) for about 67 A100-hours, but fit onset times, not R(d) ([arXiv 2610.15778](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15778)). The open slot is a Gao fit with a cheap LLM-judge proxy, plus an axis with zero proxy error. Moskovitz et al. argue that each part of a composite reward has its own "proxy point" ([arXiv 2310.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04373), not re-verified). In their terms, e-count is a component whose proxy point sits at infinity. The BoN half comes nearly free from the same base sample pool. Its weakness for awe is that "small judges get gamed" is now the expected result.

**Rank 5 is better as an axis than as a headline.** The earlier pick was a rank-1 dial. Inside the benchmark, the sharper question is whether rank 1 reaches the same distance to π* as rank 32. Rank-1 RL sufficiency has so far been shown on math rewards measured by accuracy ([LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). Keep the alpha sweep as a free local appendix. Mind the warning that one adapter's direction had only about 0.04 cosine with the overall behavior direction in the EM work ([Soligo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11618v2)).

**Rank 6 has a real gap but the worst risk-to-cost ratio.** No paper found measures planning horizon before and after RL. Nainani et al. *hypothesize* that RL strengthens planning but do not test it ([arXiv 2508.18098](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18098v1)). The trap is that probes lie. Across Qwen3, Gemma-3 and Llama-3, the upcoming rhyme was linearly decodable at line boundaries, yet only Gemma-3-27B causally used it ([Ma & Rui](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07984)). Gemma-2-2B showed no rhyme planning at all ([arXiv 2508.18098](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18098v1)). A 4B model may sit right at the threshold, which is interesting and also risky. There is a way to tie this to the spine (my inference). The optimal next-token policy of π* is the base model times a "twist": the log-probability of finishing e-free from the current prefix. That makes "planning" a defined quantity, the soft value function, and you can estimate it by rollouts. If anything is done here, probe whether RL checkpoints linearly encode that value, and add one patching test. Qwen3.5-4B inference in bf16 needs about 8 GB, so this runs on the Mac. It depends on exporting Tinker checkpoints correctly, and cookbook issue #1014 reports that PEFT silently drops the unembedding LoRA on tied-embedding models, Qwen3.5-4B included ([#1014](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/issues/1014)).

## One narrative: an exact ground truth for RL fine-tuning

The pitch is one sentence: *the lipogram is the rare RL reward where the optimum is known, so you can grade RL algorithms instead of trusting reward curves.* The write-up then runs in four short acts.

**Act 1: the target is fixed and the prediction is registered.** From a few thousand base samples on the Mac-side scorer, estimate log Z per prompt, the effective sample size, and the predicted length and e-rate of π*. Publish them before training.

**Act 2: the benchmark.** Five or six short Tinker runs at one fixed β. The entrants are the starter's IS loss, GRPO with KL in the reward, GRPO with k3 in the loss, masked-teacher OPD, and the winner at rank 1. Score each with reverse KL (exact ranking, from ELBO), forward KL where target samples exist, e-rate, length, and dollars.

**Act 3: shrinkage explained.** Overlay predicted and observed length per algorithm, then attribute the excess.

**Act 4: add the judge.** Two-axis Gao curves only if budget remains.

The challenge starter already supports this. Its defaults are Qwen3.5-4B, LoRA rank 8, group size 8, max_tokens 200, IS loss and an `advantages` array, with KL via `compute_logprobs` listed as a Hard extension ([tinker-challenge](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)).

Three design choices decide whether the numbers mean anything. First, **π* must use the same β as the training KL coefficient**, or every run is scored against an optimum it was never aiming at. GRPO without KL has no π*, so score it against a β sweep and report the β it lands nearest. Second, **the hard constraint makes Z vanishingly small for 200-token answers**, so rejection sampling for exact target samples only works on short answers. Run the forward-KL and upper-bound part with max_tokens around 32 to 64, or use a moderate soft λ where the base-sample weights stay usable, and report ESS. Third, define e_count on the decoded string, decide uppercase "E" up front, and whitelist ASCII. AprAD's lookalike-character failures show what a regex-only reward invites ([AprAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01103v2)).

On cost, the earlier report found no per-step Tinker price. The README says one quickstart run costs no more than $5 ([tinker-challenge](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)), and Inkling-Small output costs $1.20 per 1M tokens ([Inkling-Small](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-small/)). Treat this split as a plan to revise after the first measured run:

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Base sample pool and logprobs | about $2 |
| Five or six benchmark runs at about 100 steps | about $12 |
| ELBO and forward-KL scoring | about $2 |
| BoN judge scoring and two gold-judge passes for Act 4 | about $4 |
| Reserve | about $5 |

Kill criteria come first. If the first run costs over $4, drop Act 4. If the base-sample ESS is tiny at the chosen λ, shorten max_tokens before spending anything on training.

## Where this breaks

The main technical risk is loose bounds. Reverse-KL *rankings* are exact, but if the log Z bounds are many nats apart, the absolute numbers and the forward direction stay vague. The twisted-SMC infilling task had a reverse KL of 13.27 for its best method, so large values are normal ([Twisted SMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). The masked-proposal estimator is unbiased but can have huge variance on long answers. Short answers are the hedge.

A second risk is the target itself. π* is "what the base model says when tilted away from e". Fluency is inherited from the base model, not judged, so the benchmark says nothing about answer quality. Act 4 exists to cover that. A skeptic will also say KL to π* is not what users want. The answer is that it is what the objective claims to optimize, and measuring that claim is the point.

The evidence base has gaps too. Several sources here are 2026 preprints read only from abstracts or search snippets, and Korbak 2022, Moskovitz 2023 and the GFlowNet model sizes were not verified this round. Absence of prior work rests on abstract-level searches. A week of lit-checking on full texts (A*-PO, arXiv 2512.18730, arXiv 2610.08963) is cheap insurance before claiming "first" ([A*-PO](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20686); [EBM lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18730); [KLPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08963)).

## Conclusion

The shift since the earlier rounds is that the lipogram's value is no longer the e-free text, or even the adapter. It is that the task makes RL-as-inference *falsifiable* at 4B scale for a few dollars. The headline results write themselves either way: GRPO lands N nats from its optimum, OPD gets there 10x cheaper but to the wrong place, and most of the shrinkage everyone called reward hacking was the objective doing its job. Each is a number nobody has. For an infra engineer this is also the right kind of novelty. It comes from building a precise harness and holding every algorithm to the same exact ruler, not from inventing a new trick.
