# Distillation, test-time vs train-time compute, and reward overoptimization for a lipogram RL task (scan date 2026-11-16)

Context: RL fine-tune Qwen3.5-4B on Tinker to answer without the letter "e". The e-count is an exact ground-truth reward; an Inkling-Small LLM judge is the quality proxy. Budget about $25.

Note on verification: items marked [fetched] were read on the primary page this session. Items marked [search-only] come from search-result summaries. Items marked [not verified this session] are from prior knowledge, with the canonical URL, and should be checked before citing in a write-up.

## Q1. Reward model overoptimization: functional forms, follow-ups, LLM-judge replications, and an exact gold

### Takeaway
Gao et al. (2022) give closed forms for gold reward vs d = sqrt(KL): quadratic-in-d for best-of-n and d·log(d) for RL. The 2026 LLM-judge studies show judge hacking clearly, but none I found fits the Gao forms with a cheap proxy judge and a stronger gold judge. That leaves the experiment open. The lipogram adds a new element: one reward component (e-count) is exact, so you can measure overoptimization of the judge proxy while the other axis has no proxy error at all.

### Cited Findings
- Gao, Schulman, Hilton (OpenAI, arXiv Oct 2022; ICML 2023). BoN: R_bon(d) = d(α_bon − β_bon·d). RL: R_RL(d) = d(α_RL − β_RL·log d). d := sqrt(KL(π‖π_init)). BoN KL is computed analytically as log n − (n−1)/n. [fetched] — [Gao et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)
- Setup: GPT-3-series policies (1.2B main, 6B comparison), proxy RMs from 3M to 3B, a 6B gold RM. Synthetic labels were deterministic (the higher gold score wins). Sampled labels gave noisier results. [fetched] — [Gao et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)
- Coefficients scale smoothly with RM size. For RL, α_RL can be held constant across RM sizes, which leaves a clean β_RL scaling curve. The KL penalty mostly acts like early stopping: gold score depends on KL distance, not on the penalty coefficient. The authors flag this as possibly hyperparameter sensitive. [fetched] — [Gao et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)
- Rafailov et al. (arXiv Jun 2024; NeurIPS 2024). DPO, IPO and SLiC at 1B, 2.8B and 6.9B degrade at high KL budgets the way classic RLHF does, often before one epoch finishes, even with no explicit RM. [search-only] — [arXiv 2406.02900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02900)
- Coste, Anwar, Kirk, Krueger (arXiv Oct 2023; ICLR 2024). Extends the Gao setup with 25% label noise. Worst-case (WCO) and uncertainty-weighted (UWO) ensemble objectives practically eliminate BoN overoptimization, with up to 70% better BoN gold performance. For PPO, ensembles always reduce overoptimization, and a small KL penalty prevents it at no cost. [search-only] — [ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/dda7f9378a210c25e470e19304cce85d-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Moskovitz et al., "Confronting Reward Model Overoptimization with Constrained RLHF" (arXiv Oct 2023; ICLR 2024). Composite rewards (several RMs) each have a "proxy point" past which more optimization hurts gold. The method uses constrained RL with Lagrange multipliers to keep each component near its proxy point. This is directly analogous to the lipogram's e-count plus judge composite. [not verified this session] — [arXiv 2310.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04373)
- Liu et al. (Meta and Yale, arXiv Mar 2026), "Examining Reasoning LLMs-as-Judges in Non-Verifiable LLM Post-Training". gpt-oss-120b is the gold judge, and smaller judges are trained on its labels. Policies trained against non-reasoning judges hack them. Reasoning judges give policies that do well under gold, but those policies learn adversarial outputs that also fool other judges on Arena-Hard. [fetched abstract] — [arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12246)
- A search summary of the same paper says a KL-penalty sweep did not stop hacking, and that rubrics improved static agreement but not training-time robustness. [search-only, not confirmed on the primary page] — [arXiv 2603.12246 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12246)
- Wang (arXiv Oct 2026), "Who Sets the Pace of Judge Hacking? Judge Capability versus the Policy–Judge Gap". Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B policies, GRPO on GSM8K, with frozen Qwen2.5 1.5B/3B/7B, Llama-3.2-3B and other judges. GSM8K labels are the gold control. Judge acceptance rose in every cell, but no cell gained more than about 0.04 held-out accuracy. Hacking depth correlates with judge AUC (Spearman −0.69). The paper fits no Gao-style gold-vs-KL form: it uses an accelerated-failure-time model on onset steps. Total cost was about 67 A100-hours. The paper discloses that Claude agents ran the computations and drafted it. [fetched] — [arXiv 2610.15778](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15778)
- Other 2026 judge-hacking preprints seen only as titles: "LLM-as-a-Judge Is Not an Oracle" ([arXiv 2609.02246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02246)), "More Convincing, Not More Correct: Self-Play Reward Hacking of Reference-Free LLM Judges" ([arXiv 2607.05904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05904)), "Evaluator Ensembles Under Reward Hacking" ([arXiv 2608.08002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08002)), "Gradient Regularization Prevents Reward Hacking" ([arXiv 2602.18037](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.18037)), and "Debate Training Reduces Reward Hacking in RLAIF" ([arXiv 2608.17776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17776)). [search-only]
- Lipogram prior art: Balasubramanian, Gokulakrishnan, Saba, Skiena, "Gatsby Without the 'E'" (arXiv May 2025; IJCNLP-AACL 2025). They used constrained beam search with e-tokens banned through bad_words_ids, plus a T5 with an e-penalty loss. LLM methods reached 0% e. Fidelity decays "rapidly and predictably" as more letters are banned, but banning the most common letters up to 'u' had little effect on meaning. [search-only] — [arXiv 2505.20501](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20501v2); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-short.18)

### Inferences
- No source found fits Gao's R(d) forms with an LLM judge as proxy and a stronger LLM judge (or humans) as gold, at small cost. Wang (2026) comes closest on budget (67 A100-hours) but fits onset times, not R(d). A Tinker-scale Gao replication with judge proxy, stronger-judge gold and exact e-gold looks open.
- The lipogram allows a "two-axis Gao plot": an exact gold for constraint satisfaction (e-rate, with zero proxy error) and a proxy/gold pair for quality (Inkling-Small judge vs a stronger judge or a small human panel). This can separate overoptimization of the judge from "capability tax" caused by the hard constraint. In Moskovitz's terms, the e-count is a component whose proxy point is at infinity.
- A cheap version of a Gao-style curve is BoN over base-model samples scored by the judge. KL is analytic (log n − (n−1)/n), so you get a whole gold-vs-d curve from one large sample pool with no training. That fits a $25 budget.
- The exact e-count also gives a hack detector. Predictable judge-exploiting failure modes include terse answers, refusals, misspellings or substituting letters (e.g. "3" for "e"), and non-English words. These can be flagged with regexes, giving labeled hacking events cheaply.
- Fit both R_RL(d) = d(α − β log d) and R_bon(d) = d(α − β d) to the judge-quality axis. Check whether the Gao forms hold for an LLM-judge proxy (an untested claim) and whether α/β vary with the KL penalty or only with d.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports exact fitted α/β values for LLM-judge proxies.
- I could not check whether Liu et al. 2026 plots gold vs proxy against KL. Only the abstract was read.
- Moskovitz details, and the claim that Gao's BoN KL formula is only an upper bound (Beirami et al.), are not verified this session.
- Inkling-Small's judge reliability and per-call cost were not researched (out of scope).

## Q2. On-policy distillation, context distillation, self-distillation, and distilling inference-time search into a policy

### Takeaway
On-policy distillation (OPD) is natively supported on Tinker and is the right primitive. A "teacher" can be the same model given an avoid-e prompt, or an e-masked or search-augmented sampler, which makes this a context or search distillation. SMC/BoN-to-policy amortization exists (BOND, self-distilled twisted SMC), but none I found measures a compute-matched Pareto curve on an exactly scored constraint.

### Cited Findings
- Thinking Machines "On-Policy Distillation" (Kevin Lu et al., Oct 27 2025). The student samples, the teacher scores each token, and the per-token advantage is the negative reverse KL with discount 0. It is implemented as a one-line change to Tinker RL code using `compute_logprobs`. [fetched] — [TML blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Same post: AIME'24 went from 60% to 70% in about 150 steps. That is about 9–30x cheaper than extrapolated SFT, and the Qwen3 report shows OPD reaching 74.4% in 1,800 GPU hours vs RL reaching 67.6% in 17,920 GPU hours. Personalization: OPD recovered IF-eval to 83% while keeping 41% internal QA. A June 2026 update says the cookbook now uses Qwen3.5-9B-Base (student) and Qwen3.5-9B (teacher). [fetched] — [TML blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Shenfeld, Damani, Hübotter, Agrawal (MIT/ETH, arXiv Jan 2026), "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning" (SDFT). A demonstration-conditioned copy of the model is the teacher for its own unconditioned self, trained on-policy. It accumulates skills sequentially without regression, and beats offline SFT on the same teacher outputs. — [arXiv 2601.19897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897) [search-only]
- SDFT repo correction (dated 04/07/26): all paper results used on-policy sampling with per-token forward KL, not the reverse KL that secondary sources describe. — [GitHub idanshen/Self-Distillation](https://github.com/idanshen/Self-Distillation) [search-only]
- A secondary source reports that SDFT costs about 2.5x the FLOPs and 4x the wall-clock time of SFT. — [ArXivIQ](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/self-distillation-enables-continual) [search-only, secondary]
- Related self-distillation lines are OPSD and SDPO, plus "Rethinking On-Policy Self-Distillation for Thinking Models" (2026). — [Turing Post](https://www.turingpost.com/p/opdzeitgeist); [arXiv 2607.05184](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05184) [search-only]
- Kim, Nam, Park, Lee (SNU/KAIST, arXiv Jul 2025, v2 Nov 2025), "Self-Distilled Twisted SMC". Iterates three steps: TSMC sampling, MLE fine-tuning of the base model on those samples, and twist retraining. Proposal KL falls from 7.97 to 7.03 to 7.02 over generations. Rejection sampling at β=10 accepts only 0.06%. Only TinyStories-33M on a toxicity target was used, and there is no inference-compute comparison between SMC and the distilled policy. [fetched] — [arXiv 2507.02315](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02315)
- Loula et al. (ICLR 2025), "Syntactic and Semantic Control of LLMs via SMC". SMC steering corrects the bias of greedy token masking. A 2026 paper notes that locally constrained decoding (masking) yields biased samples, and that SMC convergence is bottlenecked by proposal quality. — [Loula et al.](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/papers/loula+al.iclr25.pdf); [arXiv 2606.01926](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01926v1) [search-only]
- Sessa et al. (Google DeepMind, arXiv Jul 2024; ICLR 2025), BOND. Distills the BoN distribution into a single-sample policy via online distribution matching with Jeffreys divergence (forward + reverse KL, β=0.5 best). Iterative J-BOND uses a moving anchor. Tested on Gemma for summarization. — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/947f37882a394140f7add476bb99d1d3-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2407.14622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14622v1) [search-only]
- Gui et al. (2024), BoNBoN alignment: BoN is essentially optimal on the win-rate-vs-KL trade-off, and they fine-tune to mimic the BoN distribution. — [arXiv 2406.00832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00832) [search-only title]
- On-policy distillation with best-of-N teacher rollout selection (2026) improves the student but costs extra teacher samples per prompt. — [arXiv 2605.09725](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09725v1) [search-only]
- Counter-evidence: in FASTER (2026), distilling the selected best action into a policy did substantially worse than value-guided sampling, which the authors attribute to a moving target. — [arXiv 2604.19730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19730) [search-only]
- Agarwal et al., GKD (ICLR 2024): on-policy student samples with a generalized divergence to the teacher. Snell, Klein, Zhong (2022), context distillation: train the model without the prompt to match itself with the prompt. [not verified this session] — [GKD arXiv 2306.13649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649); [Context distillation arXiv 2209.15189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15189)

### Inferences
- Candidate teachers ranked by how interesting the result would be: (a) the same Qwen3.5-4B prompted "avoid e" (context distillation, cheapest); (b) the same model with e-tokens masked at the logit level (an exact but myopic teacher whose logprobs are trivially computable, but known to be biased per Loula); (c) masking plus SMC or lookahead resampling (an unbiased-in-the-limit teacher, but with no per-token teacher logprob for OPD, so it would need BOND-style or MLE distillation instead); (d) RL with e-reward plus judge reward. A head-to-head of (a)–(d) at matched Tinker dollars is new as far as this scan found.
- The masked-logit teacher is a neat trick for OPD: teacher logprobs equal the base logprobs renormalized over e-free tokens. Reverse KL to that teacher equals the student's log-mass on e-free tokens plus a KL term, so the task becomes a closed-form distillation target. Check tokenizer handling: tokens containing "e" anywhere, including leading spaces.
- SDFT's "demonstration-conditioned self" maps onto "e-free-exemplar-conditioned self". Few-shot lipogram examples in context act as the teacher, which ties context distillation to SDFT.
- Prediction (inference, not verified): myopic masking will hurt quality the most, since it forces awkward continuations after committing to words that need an e. SMC or lookahead teachers should dominate on the judge axis. Whether distillation preserves that quality gain is the experiment's interesting question.

### Gaps
- No paper found that distills a constrained-decoding search (SMC, mask or BoN) into weights and reports a compute-matched e-rate (or constraint-rate) vs quality Pareto curve.
- "Sharpen Without Search" (2026, power-distribution SMC distillation) was seen only in a curated list and not verified. — [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation)
- Value-guided preference distillation (arXiv 2609.14648) reportedly approaches RL performance at a fraction of the cost by distilling BoN. Only a snippet was seen. — [arXiv 2609.14648](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14648)

## Q3. Test-time vs train-time compute trade-off curves

### Takeaway
Jones (2021) is the canonical exchange-rate result: about 10x train compute buys about 10x less test compute in Hex. LLM work (Snell 2024, Setlur 2025, OLMo iso-compute) shows the trade is regime-dependent. Theory says BoN is near-KL-optimal, so BoN vs RL vs distilled-BoN is a principled comparison. The lipogram's exact e-score could yield a cleaner, noise-free iso-compute frontier than any of these.

### Cited Findings
- Jones, "Scaling Scaling Laws with Board Games" (arXiv Apr 2021). For each extra order of magnitude of train-time compute, test-time compute (MCTS) can drop by a similar factor at equal performance. Compute frontiers fitted at small board sizes predict those at large sizes. — [arXiv 2104.03113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03113) [search-only]
- Snell, Lee, Xu, Kumar (arXiv Aug 2024; ICLR 2025), "Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally". Test-time compute can beat pretraining compute in some regimes, mostly on easier questions, and the two are not one-to-one exchangeable. — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1b623663fd9b874366f3ce019fdfdd44-Paper-Conference.pdf) [search-only]
- Setlur et al. (arXiv Feb 2025), "Scaling Test-Time Compute Without Verification or RL is Suboptimal". Verifier-based or RL methods scale better than verifier-free distillation of search traces. — [arXiv 2502.12118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12118) [search-only]
- Beirami et al. (arXiv Jan 2024), "Theoretical Guarantees on the Best-of-n Alignment Policy". Analyzes the BoN KL formula and compares BoN with rewind-and-repeat. BoN gets a better reward-vs-KL trade-off, while rewind-and-repeat raises reward faster per unit compute. — [arXiv 2401.01879](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01879) [search-only]
- "Best-of-N through the Smoothing Lens" (2025) gives a KL and regret analysis of BoN. — [arXiv 2507.05913](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05913) [search-only title]
- IsoCompute Playbook (2026): RL sampling compute allocation, rollouts per problem vs number of problems. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401912880_IsoCompute_Playbook_Optimally_Scaling_Sampling_Compute_for_LLM_RL) [search-only]
- sGPO (2026), "Trading Inference FLOPs for Training Efficiency in RLVR". — [arXiv 2606.08854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08854) [search-only title]
- Forethought essay: several open-weight LLMs don't benefit from spending more test-time tokens than were used in training. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/two-kinds-of-test-time-scaling) [search-only, non-academic]
- ST-BoN reportedly matches full BoN at 70–80% lower cost. — [arXiv 2503.01422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01422) [search-only]

### Inferences
- Proposed "Jones curve for language": x-axis is total compute (train FLOPs or Tinker $ + n × inference cost per query × expected queries), y-axes are exact e-rate and judge quality. Methods to compare: base + BoN(n) filtered by e-count, masked decoding, SMC with k particles, OPD-distilled policy + BoN(n), and RL policy + BoN(n). Because the e-metric is exact, the frontier has no judge noise on one axis. This is a cleaner version of Jones's exchange-rate measurement in an LLM setting.
- Headline number to aim for: "X Tinker dollars of training replace a factor of Y in test-time samples at equal e-rate and equal judge quality". Then check whether the exchange rate differs between the exact axis (e) and the proxy axis (judge). One plausible finding is that training transfers well on the exact axis but overoptimizes on the proxy axis.
- Within $25, the BoN and SMC curves come almost free from one sample pool scored offline (e-count is free; the judge is the main cost). Training runs should be a few small LoRA runs at 2–3 KL or step budgets.

### Gaps
- Snell 2024 numbers (e.g. the "4x more efficient than BoN" claim) and Setlur 2025 details were not fetched.
- No source found gives an iso-compute frontier on a constrained-generation task with an exact metric.
- Tinker pricing for Qwen3.5-4B sampling vs training was not researched, so the dollar-to-FLOP conversion remains open.
