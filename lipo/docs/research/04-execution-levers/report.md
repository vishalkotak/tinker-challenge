# Fix exploration first, then tighten the loss

The cheapest way to raise the odds is to make sure GRPO ever sees a good e-free answer. The fancier objective matters less. In order of value per dollar the levers are: mechanical reward gates (free), per-token e advantages through the built-in `importance_sampling` loss (free), BREAD-style prefix hints only on all-fail groups (about $1 to $2), and a warm-start SFT on Wu's 200 verified answers plus about 600 new short, repaired answers (about $5). Top-K unlikelihood is buildable on Tinker today and goes in as an ablation arm, not the default, because nobody has measured its fluency cost at a 28%-of-vocab ban. LUFFY mixing and Gadsby SFT drop out. One finding changes the recipe: **the per-token e penalty pays the model directly for writing "th" instead of "the"**, and Qwen 4B already falls into exactly that failure under a mask. So a dictionary gate that catches dropped e's is part of the objective, not optional cleanup. The recipe fits about $23.5 of the $25 if the round 3 rank sweep and overoptimization chapter are deferred. Revised odds: about 60% that some arm clears the 80% e-free gate at fluent length (up from 55%), about 45% for a nominal win over 60.5% usable, and about 30% for a clear win at 70% or more (down from 35%). The gate moves up because the exploration fix is now concrete. The clear win moves down because every exploration result comes from math, and Wu's own mask-free attempts collapsed.

## Mask-free RL starts at nearly zero signal, and Wu's attempts show the collapse

Wu has no mask-free success to copy. His unmasked prototypes used per-token e rewards on 8 copies of one prompt. Every sample collapsed to "i want to know what you want". Adding a KL penalty produced 7/8 e-free samples of noise such as "Tgth rthrt", and none of those configs were saved ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). His 60.5% came from a masked recipe on Qwen3-1.7B: SFT on 180 answers, then GRPO at 4 prompts x 8 samples, 300 steps, temperature 0.7, LR 2e-5, KL 0.02, reward (coherence-1 + responds-1)/8. Masked RL without SFT reached 43%, and the masked base 26% ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). Qwen3-4B scored **0% usable with the instruction alone and 12.5% under the mask** ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). So the unmasked starting rate of "e-free and good" is close to zero.

That is the regime where group-relative RL gets nothing. A group is unanimous with probability p^G + (1-p)^G ([arXiv 2607.00152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00152)). Working that through, at a per-sample success rate of 2% and G=16, 72% of groups have no success at all. At 5% the figure is 44%, and at 10% it is 19%. DAPO's fix, oversampling and dropping groups at 0 or 1 accuracy, just throws away most of the batch when p is near zero ([DAPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14476)). This math sets the first job: push the unmasked pass rate to about 10% before RL, and inject successes into the groups that still have none.

No paper trains an LLM with RL to avoid a letter. A 2026 benchmark finds that most models break lipogram constraints in earnest ([arXiv 2608.12426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12426)). Everything below borrows from math RLVR, and its transfer to open chat with a judge reward is inference.

## Exploration: hints on failure, graded reward, hard gates

**Prefix hints are the strongest exploration lever and the easiest to build.** BREAD inserts a short expert prefix only when every trace in a group fails. That guarantees one success per update. It reports needing under 40% of the ground-truth traces, training about 3x faster than GRPO, and solving problems SFT+RL cannot ([BREAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17211v1)). QuestA prepends the first p% of a solution. It ran 100 steps at p=50%, then 1,900 at p=25%, with group 16, LR 2e-5, temperature 1.0, no KL and no hints at eval. It matched a 2K-step baseline in about 1.1K steps and kept pass@k at large k ([QuestA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13266v3)). On Tinker the prefix tokens go in the assistant turn as context with advantage 0, so the datums stay on-policy and the stock loss works ([Tinker losses](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/index.md)). One honest caveat about "decay to 0": QuestA found **a 0% training stage added no gains** ([QuestA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13266v3)). Taking the decay to 0 here is a check that the policy stands on its own, not a source of lift. Hints also suit this task. BREAD's failure case, expert traces too hard for a small model to express, is real when a frontier model writes e-free vocabulary the 4B rarely uses ([BREAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17211v1)). Hints sidestep that better than full-trace imitation.

**LUFFY is the lever to skip.** It puts 1 off-policy trace in each group of 8 and reshapes the off-policy token probabilities with f(x) = x/(x+0.1). It beat SFT+RL on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (50.1 vs 48.2 in-distribution) and kept training on hard subsets where on-policy reward hit zero ([LUFFY](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945v5)). The catch is its own ablation. **Without the reshaping, mixed traces give fast early gains, then entropy collapses faster than on-policy RL and the model converges back to it** ([LUFFY](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945v5)). On Tinker, dropping a demo into a group through `importance_sampling` with a made-up sampler logprob gives exactly that unshaped variant. The reshaping needs `forward_backward_custom`, and how LUFFY's code sets the behavior logprob is unverified. Hints buy the same signal with no custom code.

**For zero-variance groups, use graded reward, not filtering alone.** Tinker's defaults are group 4, Dr. GRPO mean-centering without std normalization, and `remove_constant_reward_groups=False` ([Tinker RL hyperparams](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tutorials/advanced/rl-hyperparams/index.md)). Raise G to 16 and set the flag True. A graded reward of judge_score x max(0, 1 - k x e_count) gives near-misses variance inside a group without a threshold schedule. Cap the e term so a short e-free string can't beat a good answer with one e, which is the shortcut Wu's prototype found. Avoid the fixed-reference advantage 2r-1 proposed for degenerate groups ([arXiv 2605.07689](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.07689)). It pushes down every all-fail sample, fluent near-misses included, and that is the fastest road to letter streams.

**Gates block the known hacks, and KL won't.** With a 72B generative judge, policy length fell below 30 tokens early in training, and non-word "master keys" fooled the judge ([arXiv 2507.08794](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08794v1)). GRPO against an LLM judge overfit it while a gold judge saw quality drop, and KL to the original policy did not help ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12246)). KL did not stop Wu's collapse either ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). So reward is 0 unless the text is ASCII-only, clears a minimum length, has under 5% out-of-dictionary words, passes the dropped-e check and has no repeated n-gram loop. Only then does the judge score count. Track median length, distinct-n and a held-out gold judge every 25 steps. Wu's gain mostly arrived by step 125 of 300 ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)), so short runs with frequent evals cost little.

## Dense objectives: per-token advantages now, top-K unlikelihood as a test

**Per-token e advantages cost nothing.** Tinker's `loss_fn_inputs` take per-token `advantages`, so set advantage_t = A_seq - lambda x 1[token t contains e or E] and train with stock `importance_sampling` or `ppo` (installed 0.29.1 `datum.py`; [Tinker losses](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/index.md)). Dense credit assignment has support: RTO's token-level reward beat sequence-level PPO by 7.5 on AlpacaEval 2 ([arXiv 2404.18922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18922v4)). The weakness is that the penalty only touches e-tokens the model actually sampled. It also has a hazard. Under the mask, **Qwen3-4B wrote "th" for "the" in 51/200 answers versus 1/200 for 1.7B, with misspellings flagged in 130/200** ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). The "th" token has no e, so a per-token penalty rewards the truncation. The dropped-e gate catches it: flag any word that becomes a dictionary word when an e is inserted.

**Top-K unlikelihood is expressible but approximate.** No custom loss sees full logits. Every loss receives logprobs gathered at `target_tokens`, which may be `(N, K)` ([custom loss docs](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/custom/index.md); [SDFT recipe](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/cookbook/recipes/sdft/)). The working recipe teacher-forces each rollout through the sampler with `topk_prompt_logprobs=K` (20 to 64). The cookbook's SDFT does exactly this. Then collect the e-containing ids among the top-K at each position, plus the sampled token if it has an e. Then compute L = -sum_t log(1 - sum_c p(c) + eps) in torch. Shipping all 69k e-ids per position would mean about 280 MB of `target_tokens` per 512-token datum, so the full set is off the table. The bounded log(1-p) form matters, because plain gradient ascent on e-tokens is unbounded and tends to wreck the model ([Welleck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04319)).

**On SDK versions,** installed 0.29.1 has only the two-pass `forward_backward_custom`. It runs a server forward, your loss in local torch, then sends signed `-grad` back as `(N, K)` weights. That costs about 1.5x the FLOPs and up to 3x the wall time, and a failed op poisons the client until you resume from a checkpoint ([custom loss docs](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/custom/index.md)). SDK 0.39 adds an alpha `ServiceClient.create_custom_loss(fn, input_ranks)`. It traces `fn` with `torch.fx` (torch and math ops only, no data-dependent branching) and runs it server-side in a single pass ([service_client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker/main/src/tinker/lib/public_interfaces/service_client.py); [CustomLossGraph](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/api-reference/types/customlossgraph/index.md)). The op allowlist, the maximum K and whether rank-2 targets work with built-in RL losses are all undocumented. Build the UL arm on 0.29.1's path first and test 0.39 in a separate venv. Old SDKs have been rejected before ([tinker-feedback #120](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-feedback/issues/120)).

**The fluency cost is the open question.** Welleck's token-level UL kept perplexity and won human preference, but its negative sets were small and context-dependent ([Welleck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04319)). UL-style penalties over-penalize acceptable content in detox work ([COUNT](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.579.pdf)). And lipogram fidelity "rapidly and predictably decays" as the ban grows ([arXiv 2505.20501](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20501v2)). The ban here covers 27.9% of the vocabulary. Tail mass also matters more than it looks. A per-token e-mass of 0.001 still taints about 18% of 200-token answers at sampling time, since 0.999^200 is 0.82. Reaching 98% e-free needs about 1e-4. Top-K UL ignores the mass outside the top K, so log 1 - sum_topK p as a bound. Anchor the arm with the KL term or a small MLE loss on e-free text, as targeted negative training suggests ([TNT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.13660)).

## Data: short repaired answers, not Gadsby, and no eval leakage

**Gadsby is already a failed experiment.** Wu ran SFT on 48 Gadsby chunks of up to 2,048 tokens. Validation loss fell from 4.09 to 3.24, yet the adapter scored **24.5% usable, below the 26% masked base** ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). The 1939 text also slips: "the" appears on pp. 51, 103 and 124 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadsby_(novel))). Use it only to mine e-free vocabulary for repair hints. The Mac's /usr/share/dict/words and the google-10000 e-free subset (4,271 words) cover the dictionary gate.

**Reuse Wu's assets.** His MIT repo has 200 hand-verified e-free answers, a 1,235-prompt RL pool already filtered for answerability with keep and drop reasons, the 50-prompt eval and 25 dropped eval prompts ([repo](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj)). The prompts come from No Robots (CC BY-NC 4.0) and Dolly (CC BY-SA 3.0), so the release stays non-commercial ([No Robots](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceH4/no_robots); [Dolly](https://huggingface.co/datasets/databricks/databricks-dolly-15k)). Under the mask his SFT curve flattened: 90 examples gave 31.5%, 180 gave 35% ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). Mask-free SFT has a different job, lifting the unmasked pass rate above roughly 1/G, so test the size and don't assume it. SPEAR saw Qwen3-4B peak at about a 30% SFT share and fall beyond it, from lost exploration diversity ([arXiv 2608.26550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26550)).

**New answers need short targets and targeted repair.** GPT-6-class subagents failed all 9 first drafts of 2k-token e-free passages, with 3 of 9 accepted after repairs, at an estimated $0.49 to $2.15 each ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). GPT-4o rewriting still leaked 5.94% e ([arXiv 2505.20501](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20501v2)). Feedback loops plateau. COLLIE's GPT-4 rose from 50.9% to about 66% after three feedback rounds, "comparable to pass@5" ([COLLIE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.08689)). Self-Refine averages climbed 29.0, 40.3, 46.7, 49.7 and specific feedback beat generic feedback ([Self-Refine](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17651)). Self-correction works when the feedback is reliable and external ([Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01297)), and a regex is as reliable as feedback gets. So target answers of 80 to 250 words. Have the teacher rewrite Qwen3.5-4B's own unconstrained answer, since Qwen-sourced targets had lower SFT loss on one example (inconclusive). Run at most 2 to 3 repair rounds that list each offending word with a dictionary e-free swap, then resample or drop. Repairs must swap whole words ("the" to "this", "that" or "a") and never truncate, or the SFT set teaches the 4B habit.

**Cost per accepted answer is an estimate until a pilot measures it.** At Tinker's prices, GPT-OSS-120B ($0.33/M prefill, $0.84/M sample) works out to about $0.005 to $0.01 per accepted short answer. Kimi-K2.6 ($2.205/M, $5.49/M) works out to about $0.015 to $0.02, assuming 50 to 70% acceptance ([Tinker models and pricing](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/models/models_and_pricing/index.md)). So a few dollars buys hundreds of answers, not thousands. Tinker sampling has no logit bias or banned-token parameter, so teacher-side masking isn't available there ([SamplingParams](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/api-reference/types/samplingparams/index.md)). The free fallback is masked local drafts on the Mac followed by a teacher fluency pass. It is slow on MPS and needs the dropped-e gate.

**Filters and leakage.** Of Wu's 95 earlier rewrites, 44 contained non-ASCII typography ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)). Reject non-ASCII after NFKD normalization, then run a case-insensitive e-check, the dictionary and dropped-e gate, a minimum length, and the judge at 4/5 or better on both axes. Validate the judge on Wu's 200 answers plus deliberately broken variants. Hold out the 50 eval prompts and their source items from both SFT and the hint pool. Wu threw away a 9-answer pilot for exactly this leak ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md)).

## The recipe, its gates and its budget

Settings come from Wu and Tinker defaults except where noted. My own picks are marked.

| Component | Setting |
|---|---|
| Model and SDK | Qwen3.5-4B LoRA rank 8; main arms on 0.29.1, 0.39.1 tested in a separate venv |
| SFT | Wu's 200 plus about 600 new answers; compare 200 vs 800; early stop on val loss; 2 to 3 epochs |
| RL batch | 4 prompts x 16 samples, max 300 tokens, temperature 0.7 (Wu; LUFFY and QuestA use 1.0, but that also samples more e's) |
| Optimizer | LR 1e-5, one retune at 2e-5; KL 0.02 as a fluency anchor, not a collapse guard |
| Reward | 0 if any gate fails; else q x max(0, 1 - 0.25 x e_count), q = (coh-1 + resp-1)/8 (my k) |
| Dense term | advantage_t = A_seq - lambda x 1[e in token], lambda about 0.1 on the reward scale, tuned in the pilot (my pick) |
| Groups | Dr. GRPO centering, `remove_constant_reward_groups=True` |
| Hints | Success = gates pass, 0 e, coh and resp at 4 or above. If a group has no success, resample it with the first p% of a verified answer as context. p=50% for steps 1 to 40, 25% for 41 to 100, 0 after |
| Judges | Inkling 2 Small for training reward; gpt-oss-120b for gold eval |

| Step | Work | Cost | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Mac: round 3 baselines, detectors, dictionary, judge validation set | $0 | Detectors flag every planted "th"/"manag" and non-ASCII case |
| 1 | Data pilot on 50 prompts | $0.5 | 50% or more accepted at $0.02 or less each, else shorten targets or switch teacher |
| 2 | Generate about 600 answers; validate the judge | $4.5 | Spot-check 30 by hand |
| 3 | SFT at 200 and 800; measure the unmasked pass rate on held-out train prompts | $1.5 | 10% or more: go. 3 to 10%: keep p=50% hints longer. Under 3%: stop and rethink |
| 4 | Arm A (SFT + hints + per-token + gates), 150 steps at about $0.05/step | $7.5 | Step 30: e-free rate up, median length within 50% of base, OOV under 5% |
| 5 | Arm B (A without hints), 60 steps | $3 | Isolates the hint lever |
| 6 | Arm C (A + top-K UL, K=32), 60 steps | $4 | Gold quality not worse than A at the same e-free rate |
| 7 | Wu-protocol evals, about 8 checkpoints at about $0.31 | $2.5 | Under 70% gold usable is a tie with Wu |
| | Reserve | $1.5 | |

The total is about **$23.5**. The step cost is my arithmetic from the published prices ($0.33 prefill, $1.005 sample, $0.737 train per M on Qwen3.5-4B, plus the judge), worst case on length. The UL arm adds a prompt-logprob pass and about 0.5x training FLOPs. Round 3's kill rule stands: if no arm reaches 80% e-free at median length within 30% of base, publish the negative result. The rank 1/8/32 sweep and the overoptimization chapter from round 3 don't fit alongside these arms. Run them only from leftover credit, or drop Arm B if hints clearly carry Arm A by step 30.

| Outcome | Round 3 | Round 4 | Why it moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Some arm at 80% or more e-free, fluent length | 55% | 60% | Hints plus gates now have a concrete answer to zero signal |
| Nominal win over 60.5% usable | not given | 45% | 4B size helps; no mask-free precedent hurts |
| Clear win, 70% or more gold usable | 35% | 30% | All exploration evidence is from math; the dropped-e hack is live; UL fluency cost unknown |

## Conclusion

Round 3 treated the objective as the main bet. The evidence here says the bottleneck sits upstream: at a pass rate near zero, no loss function has anything to push on. Hints and a modest repaired SFT set turn the problem from "find a needle" into "sharpen a known behavior", and the dense terms only help after that. The other lesson is that the dense e penalty and the 4B's misspelling habit point the same way. A model can satisfy "no e" by writing broken English, and only a dictionary gate tells the two apart. If Arm A beats Arm B by step 30, the write-up has a finding no masked recipe can produce. If Arm C matches A's quality at a higher e-free rate, top-K UL graduates from ablation to default for the next run.
