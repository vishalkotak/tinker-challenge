# Kill the benchmark, keep the dissection

The round 1 spine is dead. Ranking RL recipes by KL to the KL-regularized optimum (A) is a 2024 ICML method, and the ranking it gives is just the regularized objective at a chosen β. The absolute numbers that would be new can't be certified at lipogram strength. The challenge also isn't what round 1 assumed. The reward is a capped e-rate penalty, the judge grade is hard-coded to 0, there is no KL term, and Tinker sampling can't mask tokens ([lipogram.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)). That also breaks A2 as stated. Under an e-count target, "shrinkage is optimal" is true by construction. Under the real e-rate reward, it shrinks to a small variance effect. B, C and D all survive, but only modified, and each modification makes it smaller and more honest. The best round 3 entry merges the earlier rank-1 dial with C's cheapest test. Train mask-free with the judge on, then split the adapter into its unembedding part and its body part on the Mac. That one experiment answers three questions. Can mask-free RL beat Kevin Wu's failure? Does rank 1 suffice on a lexical reward? Is the learned policy a myopic logit filter or something that plans? D reframed as an LCD-anchored KL and B reframed as a best-of-n-first Gao fit are the backups.

## The challenge is a capped e-rate with no judge and no KL

Round 1 built on three wrong premises. Here is what the official starter does. Base model `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B`, LoRA rank 8, group size 8, **10 training steps**, max_tokens 200, learning rate 8e-4, temperature 1.0, thinking off. The reward is **`-min(1.5, e_rate/0.10)`**, where e_rate is the count of "e" over the count of alphabetic characters. The `JUDGE_WEIGHT * grade/5` term exists, but **grade is hard-coded to 0** because the judge call is commented out. Advantages are group-mean, std-normalized and broadcast to every token, under `loss_fn="importance_sampling"` ([lipogram.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)). KL against the base appears only as a "Hard" extension with no coefficient ([README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)), so **β = 0 by default** and default runs have no π*. The README itself says the starter outputs "degrade into gibberish." The script also reads a `LEADERBOARD_NAME` env var, so some leaderboard exists, which corrects the earlier "no leaderboard" note.

On the Tinker side, **`SamplingParams` has six fields: max_tokens, seed, stop, temperature, top_k and top_p.** There is no logit_bias, bad-words list or logit processor ([SamplingParams](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/api-reference/types/samplingparams/index.md)). `compute_logprobs` and the top-k and target-token logprob options do exist, but full-vocab logits are not documented ([SamplingClient](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/api-reference/samplingclient/index.md)). Every mask baseline and masked-proposal estimator from round 1 therefore has to run locally or token by token. On Tinker, mask-free RL isn't a choice, it's the only option.

Two export traps apply to every local analysis. The docs disagree on whether `sampler_weights` can be downloaded, and the safe path is `save_weights_external` ([weights docs](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tutorials/core-concepts/weights/)). Cookbook issue #1014 (open since 2026-11-15) shows that on tied-embedding models, Qwen3.5-4B included, the unembedding LoRA is mapped onto `embed_tokens`. PEFT only warns, and the logits then **match the base model exactly** ([#1014](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/issues/1014)). One more open fact: aggregators describe both an Inkling-Small and a newer Inkling 2 Small (594B MoE, 17B active, 2026-11-05), so check which judge the challenge exposes ([letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/thinking-machines-lab-releases-inkling-2-small-a-594b-moe-mo-2b2a96ce)).

## Verdicts: one kill, five modified survivors

| Candidate | Verdict | Decisive evidence | What survives |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. KL-to-optimum benchmark | **Kill** | Zhao et al. already report bidirectional KL to target for PPO, DPG and twisted SMC with SMC log Z sandwiches ([arXiv 2404.17546](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). Korbak et al. did KL to an EBM for REINFORCE, Ziegler and DPG on word constraints ([arXiv 2206.00761](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.00761v2)). log Z is constant across recipes, so the ranking equals E[r] - β·KL, a point on the standard frontier. Exact target samples need roughly e^-12 to e^-96 acceptance | A β-sweep plot showing where recipe rankings cross, under the real e-rate reward with explicit K1-in-reward KL ([Comedy of Estimators](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21852)). A footnote, not a project |
| A2. Shrinkage is optimal | **Modify, low priority** | Tautological under e-count. Under the real rate reward, length isn't penalized directly. The only objective-side push is variance: short answers have noisier rates and the exponential tilt rewards variance. That is tiny at about 750 letters. The optimizer side is covered by Dr. GRPO and length-invariance work ([arXiv 2510.04474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04474v2)) | One chart. Measure base cap-hit rate at 200 tokens, then split the length drop with a Dr. GRPO-style ablation. Learned EOS replacing truncation is the likely real story |
| B. Two-axis Gao curves | **Modify** | Liu et al. ran proxy-judge vs gold-judge GRPO, but plotted against steps and fit no Gao forms ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12246)). Wang fit onset correlations, not R(d) ([arXiv 2610.15778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15778)). Hacked outputs transfer across judges, so an LLM gold isn't ground truth. One proxy gives one curve, not a law | Lead with the exact e-rate axis as the only true gold. Best-of-n first (analytic KL log n - (n-1)/n), one RL run with checkpoints, and vary proxy strength by reasoning effort or e-weight instead of model size ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)) |
| C. RL extends planning horizon | **Modify** | Open slot. No paper probes planning horizon across RL checkpoints. But Ma & Rui show plans that probes can decode are often causally unused, and k-ahead probes hit the unigram baseline by k=8 ([arXiv 2605.07984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07984)). Under e-only reward, the policy's own text has almost no e-words, so labels go constant. 10 steps on 8 prompts is too little signal | Pre-registered contrast: e-only RL shows no horizon change, e-plus-judge RL shows a small k=1-2 gain. Fixed teacher-forced corpus, AUC deltas with bootstrap CIs, Hewitt-Liang controls, a causal steering arm |
| D. Masked-teacher OPD | **Modify** | Tinker can't mask, but it doesn't need to. With zero discount, -log Z_t depends only on the prefix and drops out of the expected gradient, so the teacher is base logprobs with e-tokens clipped. Its fixed point is the LCD distribution, which is known to be myopic and biased ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21047); [Lipkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05410)). With full discount it collapses into KL-regularized RL. Closest prior art, CC-OPD, conditions the teacher on the prompt rather than masking it ([arXiv 2609.27421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27421)) | LCD-masked base as a per-token KL anchor with the judge as reward, against plain-base-KL RL and pure OPD-to-LCD at matched dollars. Does the anchor stop pulling the policy back toward "e"? |
| Rank-1 adapter dial | **Survive, merged** | Rank-1 RL sufficiency has only been shown on math ([LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). One rank-1 adapter's direction had about 0.04 cosine with the overall behavior direction ([Soligo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11618v2)). #1014 would silently zero the most likely carrier of the effect | Rank 1 vs rank 8 or 32 mask-free, alpha sweep including negative, and the unembed-vs-body decomposition below |
| Mask-free RL vs Wu | **Survive, foundation** | Wu: RL without a mask didn't remove "e". Mask plus GRPO reached 43% usable and SFT plus GRPO reached 60.5% on Qwen3-1.7B. Qwen3-4B under the mask did worse (12.5%) by dropping letters ([Wu](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). The starter's per-token advantage extension is untested in public. Tinker forces mask-free anyway | Per-token negative advantages on e-tokens, judge on, length tracked from step one |

The reason A dies matters more than the citations do. KL(π‖π\*) = log Z - [E_π r/β - KL(π‖p_base)], and log Z is the same for every recipe. Without log Z you get a ranking anyone can already compute. With log Z you hit the infeasible part. A 200-token answer has about 750 letters and about 90 e's. Hard zero-e acceptance under the base is roughly 0.88^750, near e^-96. Matching what RL reaches under a soft rate target means a 5-8 standard deviation shift, which costs 1e5 to 1e14 base samples for each exact target sample. These are my back-of-envelope numbers, but they agree with Zhao et al., whose simpler threshold task already "needed hundreds of thousands of rejection samples" ([arXiv 2404.17546](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). Round 1's claim that rejection sampling works for indicator targets is true only at max_tokens around 32, where the task stops being a lipogram.

## The merged experiment separates a filter from a planner

C's sharpest fix and the rank-1 dial's per-layer ablation are the same experiment. Under the default reward, logit-level suppression of e-tokens can solve most of the task myopically. That suppression lives naturally in the unembedding LoRA, the exact piece #1014 drops. So train mask-free with per-token advantages and the judge on. Export with `save_weights_external`, patch #1014 to map `unembed_tokens` to `lm_head`, and before any analysis check that local logprobs match Tinker's sampler on the same tokens. Then run three things on the Mac. First, ablate unembed-only vs body-only. If unembed-only reproduces most of the e-rate drop, the policy is a filter and the horizon claim is dead. That's a clean, publishable answer to "what does RL learn here". Second, sweep alpha from negative to positive for e-rate, length and fluency. Third, project the residual-writing B vectors through the unembedding and compare e vs non-e tokens.

Run one more arm with e-only reward, which is the starter as shipped. The prediction to register: e-only gives a filter with no horizon change and gibberish. E-plus-judge pushes weight into the body and possibly a small gain at k=1-2. Wu's account fits that prediction. A count-only reward produced streams of letters, and 4B dropped letters instead of switching words ([Wu](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn/)). Qwen3.5-4B fits in about 10 GB in bf16. Its Gated DeltaNet layers fall back to slow pure PyTorch on MPS, but 200-token teacher-forced passes still take seconds ([Unsloth PR #5434](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/pull/5434); [config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B/raw/main/config.json)). One caveat: the base was pretrained with a 1-layer MTP head, so lookahead at small k probably starts high and leaves little headroom.

## Conclusion

The lipogram's exact reward is still the asset. What changed is where it pays off. It doesn't make RL-as-inference checkable at 4B, because the partition function is out of reach exactly where recipes differ. It does give an exact, per-token label for dissecting what a small adapter learned. The strongest claim within reach for $25 is mechanistic, not distributional: under the shipped reward, RL learns a vocabulary filter in the unembedding, and only a quality signal forces it into the body. That claim is cheap to falsify, it ties directly to the lab's own rank-1 and on-policy distillation posts, and it lands either way. The other lesson is about process. Two rounds of plausible ranking were overturned by reading 200 lines of the starter script, so round 3 should start by running it once and measuring.
