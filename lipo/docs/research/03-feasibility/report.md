# Ship a mask-free lipogram, then dissect it

Accept the steer, with four amendments. The pick is one project in two chapters. Chapter 1 trains the first mask-free lipogram model: Qwen3.5-4B on Tinker with per-token negative advantages on e-tokens plus a judge reward, at rank 1 vs 8 vs 32. It is scored on Kevin Wu's released protocol against his re-judged adapters, and the adapter is then dissected on the Mac. Chapter 2 reuses those checkpoints for an overoptimization study on the exact e-rate axis, with gpt-oss-120b as a different-family gold judge and best-of-n run first. The hack catalog falls out of both chapters for free. Headline: **"Without a decoding mask, RL teaches Qwen3.5-4B to write without 'e' better than masked decoding does, and a $25 ablation shows where in the adapter that skill lives."** Round 3 changes four things. The headline judge must be gpt-oss-120b, not the training judge. The fair baseline is Qwen3.5-4B under a local mask, because Wu's 60.5% is a 1.7B model. "Filter vs planner" has to be stated as "where the change lives", with the masked base as the yardstick for a perfect myopic filter. And the analysis depends on patching cookbook issue #1014, not on its easy workaround. Every price and API needed is verified. Rank 1 is the one open item, and one free API call settles it. The compute fits about $22 with a $3 reserve. My honest odds: about 55% that mask-free clears 80% e-free at fluent length, about 35% that it beats Wu by a statistically clear margin, and well under that for direct engagement from the lab.

## The steer survives, minus three overclaims

The core bet is sound and still unclaimed. Wu's notes record **no controlled mask-free RL run with the judge as reward**. His early mask-free prototypes collapsed, their configs were never kept, and every number he reports, the 4B row included, uses the decode-time mask ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md); [Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Tinker's `SamplingParams` has only max_tokens, seed, stop, temperature, top_k and top_p, so on Tinker mask-free is the only option, not a design choice ([SamplingParams](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/api-reference/types/samplingparams/index.md)). Wu's failures are on record: e-only reward gave letter streams and stock sentences, and the mask alone gave "riddiculous" ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). So a mask-free model that reads cleanly is a real first.

Three parts of the steer overclaim. First, "beating 60.5%" is cross-size and cross-generation. Wu trained **Qwen3-1.7B**, which scores 80.5% on coherence and responsiveness with no rule at all. Qwen3-4B scores 87.5% unconstrained and only **12.5% under the mask**, worse than 1.7B's 26% ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Beating his number with a bigger model is partly a size effect. The fix costs nothing: generate Qwen3.5-4B under a strict local mask on the Mac and report it next to the result. That baseline is the strongest possible myopic filter on the same model. Beating it is what licenses the word "mask-free beats mask".

Second, the significance bar sits above 60.5%. With n=200, Wu's paired-bootstrap 90% CIs run about ±8 points (his gain was +25.5, CI +17.5 to +33.5) ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Anything under about 70% usable is a tie, and the write-up must say so.

Third, the adapter ablation shows where the change lives, not whether the model plans. Even a body-heavy adapter can suppress "e" at the last position. Round 2 found that probe-decodable plans are often causally unused ([arXiv 2605.07984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07984)). The defensible claim has two parts. If unembedding-only reproduces most of the e-rate drop, the policy is a vocabulary filter. If the full adapter beats the masked base on usable at zero e, it does something no myopic filter can. Use "planner" only if both point that way.

A fourth amendment concerns the judge. Train on a cheap proxy (Inkling 2 Small or Inkling-Small), but report the headline on **gpt-oss-120b at high reasoning**, the gold judge Liu et al. picked because it is open, reasons visibly and is a different family from the policy ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12246)). Re-judge Wu's two released adapters under the same gold judge ([k33wu/qwen3-1.7b-no-e](https://huggingface.co/k33wu/qwen3-1.7b-no-e); [sft180](https://huggingface.co/k33wu/qwen3-1.7b-no-e-sft180)). If his SFT+RL adapter lands near 60% under gold, the judges agree. If not, report the gap and compare only within the shared judge.

### Protocol to copy exactly

Copy his protocol exactly: the first 50 of the 75 e-answerable validation prompts, 4 samples each, a 300-token cap, thinking off, temperature 0.7 (stated for RL; the eval temperature is inferred) and his system message ([experiment_history.md](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/docs/experiment_history.md); [Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Port the judge prompt verbatim from [core/judge.py](https://github.com/kevinyvv/qwen-3-1.7b-finetune-proj/blob/main/core/judge.py): Coherence and Responds on a 1 to 5 scale, pass at 4 on both, repetition capped at 3, JSON output. Usable means both scores are at least 4 **and** the text passes a strict mechanical check: ASCII only, no e or E. Wu's mask blocks non-ASCII, so a mask-free model gets no slack there ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Report median length and the count under 25 tokens next to usable, because Wu's RL shrank answers from 70 to 40 tokens and his judge gives one-word answers coherence 5 ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)).

### Training recipe

Use per-token advantages: the group-normalized judge advantage on every token, plus a fixed negative term on any token whose decoded text contains e or E. Pass them through the `advantages` array under `importance_sampling`. The starter README lists this as a Medium extension, and nobody has tested it in public ([tinker-challenge](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge)). Match Wu's step size (4 prompts x 8 samples, 300 tokens) and draw the prompts from his training pool. The prompts must stay disjoint from the 50 eval prompts.

## What died, and the hack catalog that replaces it

Round 2 killed four ideas, and their proofs still hold. The **KL-to-optimum benchmark** died first. log Z is constant across recipes, so ranking by KL(π‖π\*) equals ranking on E[r] - β·KL, which anyone can already compute. Exact target samples need roughly e^-96 acceptance at 200 tokens, and Zhao et al. already did the method ([arXiv 2404.17546](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17546)). **"Shrinkage is optimal"** is tautological under an e-count reward. Under the real rate reward only a tiny variance effect survives, and Dr. GRPO covers the optimizer side ([arXiv 2510.04474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04474v2)). **Masked-teacher OPD as pitched** collapses to the LCD distribution with zero discount, which is known to be myopic and biased, and to KL-regularized RL with full discount ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21047)). **Probe-only planning claims** fail because probe-decodable plans are often causally unused and k-ahead probes reach the unigram baseline by k=8 ([arXiv 2605.07984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07984)). That last kill is why chapter 1 uses causal ablations, not probes.

The hack catalog fills the space those ideas left. Documented hacks are look-alike swaps ([arXiv 2410.01103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01103), from a search summary only), foreign-token substitution ([prateekfr card](https://huggingface.co/prateekfr/qwen3-1.7b-no-e-lora)), letter streams, a repeated stock sentence, one-word answers, half answers, and word swaps such as "clik" for "cliché" ([Wu blog](https://kevin-wu.ca/blog/qwn)). Each has a detector that takes a few lines of code. An ASCII whitelist plus NFKD normalization catches Cyrillic е, é and full-width Ｅ. A dictionary OOV rate above about 5% catches letter streams and misspellings. Length floors and the under-25-token count catch shrinkage. Distinct-n catches loops. The digit share catches "3" for "three". The prateekfr card claims 88% usable with a mask that lowercases but does not block accented forms, and it documents no eval set or judge. That is the cautionary example of why the whitelist matters ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/prateekfr/qwen3-1.7b-no-e-lora)). Liu et al. found that a learned hack fooled even the gold LLM judge ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12246)). So the mechanical detectors are the real gold for the letter rule, and gpt-oss-120b is gold only for quality. That split is what makes chapter 2's exact e-rate axis novel: Liu plotted proxy against gold only by step and fit no Gao curves ([arXiv 2603.12246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12246); [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10760v1)).

## Package it as one repo, one chart and one email

Publish a public repo forked from tinker-challenge with the detectors, Wu-protocol eval harness, configs, raw rollouts, the local ablation scripts and cost per run. Tag the runs with `LEADERBOARD_NAME` and method metadata, since the script reads it ([lipogram.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-challenge/blob/main/lipogram.py)). The README opens on one chart: gold usable vs strict e-free rate, for Wu's two adapters, the masked 4B base, and the rank 1/8/32 arms, with CIs. Next come the ablation bar chart, the alpha dial, the overoptimization curves, and a table of hacked samples next to clean ones. Sampler checkpoints work in the Tinker Playground, so give a Playground link to the best adapter ([Manage Checkpoints](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/howto/checkpoints/index.md)). The lab's call for community projects asks for open code, baselines, an LR sweep, raw rollouts and clean labeled charts ([Call for Community Projects](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/call-for-community-projects/)).

The main risks, in order:

1. Mask-free RL fails again. Per-token penalties could still drive shrinkage or OOV drift. The Day 4 gate turns that into a published negative result with per-token evidence, which is still more than anyone has posted.
2. Judge mismatch. If gold re-judges Wu far from 60.5%, the "beats Wu" line weakens to "beats Wu under a shared judge".
3. Infra gaps. Rank 1 could be rejected, Inkling top-k logprobs are unverified, and the MPS DeltaNet fallback could make the 4B masked baseline slow.

My odds are about 55% for a mask-free arm passing the 80% e-free gate, about 35% for a statistically clear win over Wu's re-judged adapter, and about 60% that the ablation gives a clean filter-or-not answer either way. Those are judgment calls, not measurements.

## Conclusion

The project's value moved from the leaderboard number to the comparison set. A mask-free 4B beating Wu's 60.5% is a headline. Beating the masked Qwen3.5-4B base on the same judge is the actual finding, because that base is the best a myopic filter can do. The unembed vs body ablation then explains the result instead of just reporting it. Run Day 1 entirely on the Mac before spending anything. Wu's adapters, the masked baselines and the judge calibration decide whether the headline is reachable, and they cost nothing.
